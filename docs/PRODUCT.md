# Product and content governance

## Learning loop
1. Select a specialty and inspect its domains, topics, and measurable objectives.
2. Identify uncovered objectives and add vetted resources.
3. Learn from a source, then create or practice questions and flashcards.
4. Review due cards and record confidence.
5. Revisit weak or uncovered topics; repeat.

## Content record requirements
Every authored MCQ or clinical case should carry: stable ID, specialty/topic mapping, author or reviewer, source URL or textbook reference, source publication/update date, review date, difficulty, and an explanation. Clinical management content must specify jurisdiction or guideline context where relevant.

## Curriculum completeness
The included curriculum is a cross-specialty starter map. Completeness must be measured against a named source syllabus (for example, a national board, college, or institutional rotation blueprint). Track each objective as mapped, sourced, drafted, clinically reviewed, and exam-validated. Do not report a specialty as complete based only on topic counts.

## Resource integrity
Use outbound search links rather than copying protected material. Respect robots, API terms, copyright, deck licenses, and publisher licenses. A search result is not an endorsement. Display source dates and distinguish primary research, systematic reviews, guidelines, and educational content.

## Safety
Usiphu is an educational tool, not a clinical decision-support device. It must not provide patient-specific diagnosis or treatment recommendations without appropriate governance, validation, and regulatory review. Show a persistent educational disclaimer and direct learners to supervisors, current guidelines, and local protocols.
