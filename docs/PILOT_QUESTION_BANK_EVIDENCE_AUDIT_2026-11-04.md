# Pilot question-bank evidence audit — 4 November 2026

## Scope

This audit checks the 17 selected pilot MCQs already included in the Urology and General Surgery pilot banks against current specialty guidance and open-access medical sources. It is an editorial evidence audit, not a substitute for independent clinical or faculty approval.

## Evidence checks completed

### Urology (9 MCQs)

- Pregnancy imaging for suspected urolithiasis: the 2026 EAU Urolithiasis guideline identifies ultrasound as the preferred initial modality, MRI as second line and low-dose CT as a last-line option.
- Bladder cancer presentation, CT urography and cystoscopy: the 2026 EAU NMIBC diagnostic guideline supports haematuria as the most common finding, CT urography for upper-tract assessment, and cystoscopy with or without biopsy as necessary for diagnosis.
- Repeat TURBT: the 2026 EAU NMIBC guideline supports repeat resection for incomplete resection, absent detrusor muscle (with specified exceptions), and T1 disease.
- Muscle-invasive bladder cancer: the 2026 EAU MIBC guideline supports radical cystectomy with pelvic lymph-node dissection and urinary diversion as standard surgical treatment for eligible T2–T4a N0M0 disease, and trimodality therapy as maximal TURBT followed by concurrent chemoradiotherapy for selected patients.
- Renal cell carcinoma: the 2026 EAU RCC guideline supports multiphasic contrast-enhanced CT of abdomen and chest for diagnosis/staging and defines extension beyond Gerota fascia as T4.

### General Surgery (8 MCQs)

- Breast lump fixation/tethering: supported by open-access clinical examination and skin-tethering literature; the stem distinguishes fixation to pectoral structures from simple mobility or cyclical tenderness.
- Breast cancer distant metastasis: source strengthened to a breast-cancer-specific open-access review identifying bone as the most frequent metastatic site.
- Palpable breast mass at age 45: ACR Appropriateness Criteria support diagnostic mammography/tomosynthesis as usually appropriate initial imaging in women aged 40 or older.
- Medial scapular winging: evidence strengthened with an open-access anatomical review linking medial winging to serratus anterior paralysis and long thoracic nerve injury.
- Axillary levels: open-access literature supports their definition relative to pectoralis minor.
- Bloody single-duct nipple discharge: open-access reviews identify intraductal papilloma as a common and often most frequent benign cause; malignancy still requires exclusion.
- Breast carcinoma histology: NCI PDQ identifies invasive ductal carcinoma as the most common histological type (approximately 70–80%).
- Mammary Paget disease: open-access review supports the typical unilateral eczematous, erythematous or scaly nipple–areolar presentation.
